# Turaco Research — Governance Framework

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Founding and supporting organisation:** Clevaway  
**Effective date:** To be determined

---

## 1. Purpose

This Governance Framework establishes the organisational structure, decision-making processes, leadership responsibilities, accountability mechanisms and community participation procedures of Turaco Research.

Its purpose is to enable effective, transparent, inclusive and sustainable governance while protecting the initiative's founding mission and principles.

This framework shall be interpreted consistently with the Founding Charter and Clevaway Stewardship Policy.

## 2. Governance principles

Turaco Research shall operate according to the following governance principles:

- Mission-driven leadership.
- Open and inclusive participation.
- Transparent decision-making.
- Merit-based responsibility.
- Delegated operational authority.
- Meaningful language community representation.
- Scientific and technical integrity.
- Financial accountability.
- Responsible management of institutional resources.
- Fair conflict resolution.
- Leadership continuity and succession.

Governance authority shall be exercised as a responsibility towards the initiative and its community rather than as a personal entitlement.

## 3. Organisational structure

The governance structure of Turaco Research shall comprise:

- Founders.
- Steering Committee.
- Core Maintainers.
- Working Group Leads.
- Maintainers.
- Trusted Contributors.
- Contributors.
- Community Members.
- Advisors and institutional partners, where appropriate.

An individual may hold multiple roles when operationally necessary, particularly during the founding stage.

Holding multiple roles shall not grant additional votes within the same decision-making body.

## 4. Founders

### 4.1. Founding recognition

The two original founders shall retain historical recognition for establishing Turaco Research.

Founding recognition shall remain distinct from active leadership authority, administrative access and financial interests.

### 4.2. Initial authority

During the founding stage, the two founders shall constitute the interim Steering Committee.

The founders shall jointly oversee the initiative's strategic direction, institutional relationships, governance development and operational sustainability.

Routine operational responsibilities shall be exercised according to the agreed founding responsibility portfolios.

### 4.3. Limitations

Founding status shall not grant:

- Exclusive ownership of community contributions.
- Automatic entitlement to project funding.
- Unrestricted authority over community members.
- Exemption from applicable project policies.
- Permanent unilateral decision-making authority.
- Permission to circumvent established governance procedures.

### 4.4. Transition of authority

The founders shall progressively delegate operational and governance responsibilities to qualified community members.

Upon the establishment of broader community governance, the founders shall exercise leadership authority according to their active roles and the applicable governance procedures.

Any rights or obligations arising from separate legal agreements shall remain subject to those agreements and applicable law.

## 5. Steering Committee

### 5.1. Purpose

The Steering Committee shall serve as the principal strategic and governance authority of Turaco Research.

Its responsibilities shall include:

- Protecting the Founding Charter.
- Establishing strategic priorities.
- Approving major policies.
- Reviewing institutional partnerships.
- Overseeing financial governance.
- Approving the establishment of working groups.
- Appointing and reviewing Core Maintainers.
- Managing material conflicts of interest.
- Resolving escalated governance disputes.
- Supporting leadership succession.
- Reviewing the institutional relationship with Clevaway.
- Protecting the long-term continuity of the initiative.

The Steering Committee shall not unnecessarily intervene in routine technical or linguistic decisions that fall within properly delegated authority.

### 5.2. Composition

The Steering Committee shall evolve through three governance stages.

**Stage A — Founding governance**

The Steering Committee shall initially consist of the two founders.

Strategic decisions requiring committee approval shall require mutual agreement.

**Stage B — Transitional governance**

The Steering Committee may expand to three or five members through the appointment of qualified community representatives.

Appointments shall be publicly documented and subject to community consultation.

**Stage C — Established community governance**

The Steering Committee shall ordinarily consist of five or seven voting members.

Non-founder members shall constitute a majority.

The composition should provide meaningful representation of:

- Artificial intelligence research.
- Software engineering and technical infrastructure.
- Data governance and linguistic expertise.
- Language communities.
- Community development and institutional operations.

At least one voting member shall represent language-community interests and possess relevant linguistic or community experience.

No external sponsor or supporting institution shall receive an automatic voting seat solely because of its financial or institutional contribution.

### 5.3. Eligibility

Candidates for Steering Committee membership should demonstrate:

- Commitment to the Founding Charter.
- Relevant expertise or meaningful contributions.
- Responsible and constructive participation.
- Integrity and sound judgment.
- Ability to collaborate across disciplines.
- Willingness to disclose conflicts of interest.
- Availability to fulfil the responsibilities of membership.

Eligibility shall not depend exclusively on software contributions, employment status, academic qualifications or financial contributions.

### 5.4. Appointments during transitional governance

Candidates may be nominated by founders, maintainers, working groups or eligible community members.

Proposed appointments shall be published for community feedback for at least fourteen calendar days.

During the founding stage, appointments shall require the approval of both founders.

During transitional governance, appointments shall require a two-thirds majority of the eligible Steering Committee membership.

Appointment decisions and their rationale shall be publicly documented, subject to legitimate confidentiality requirements.

### 5.5. Elections under established community governance

Once established community governance is adopted, ordinary non-founder committee seats shall be filled through community elections.

Voting eligibility shall be based on documented, meaningful participation rather than exclusively on GitHub activity.

The Steering Committee shall establish a public election procedure defining:

- Voter eligibility.
- Candidate eligibility.
- Nomination procedures.
- Election administration.
- Voting methods.
- Representation requirements.
- Tie-breaking procedures.
- Election complaints and appeals.

The initial election procedure shall be approved and published before established community governance becomes effective.

The voting membership register shall be determined before each election and shall not be altered improperly to influence an electoral outcome.

### 5.6. Terms of service

Under established community governance, Steering Committee members shall serve two-year terms.

Members may serve a maximum of two consecutive full terms, followed by a minimum one-year break before returning to the committee.

Historical founding recognition shall not be affected by the expiration of a governance term.

The terms of initial transitional members may be staggered to preserve institutional continuity.

### 5.7. Chairperson

The Steering Committee shall appoint a chairperson from among its voting members.

The chairperson shall:

- Coordinate committee meetings.
- Facilitate governance discussions.
- Support the preparation of meeting agendas.
- Ensure decisions are appropriately documented.
- Monitor agreed governance actions.
- Represent the committee where authorized.

The chairperson shall not possess an additional casting vote or unrestricted executive authority.

The appointment shall ordinarily be reviewed annually.

### 5.8. Vacancies

Committee vacancies arising from resignation, inactivity, removal or other circumstances shall be filled through the applicable appointment or election procedure.

Temporary appointments may be made where necessary to maintain operational continuity.

Temporary appointments shall not be used to circumvent ordinary leadership selection procedures.

## 6. Core Maintainers

### 6.1. Purpose

Core Maintainers shall coordinate the cross-project technical direction, quality and sustainability of Turaco Research.

### 6.2. Responsibilities

Core Maintainers shall oversee:

- Technical architecture and engineering standards.
- Research reproducibility.
- Model development standards.
- Dataset and model versioning requirements.
- Technical security practices.
- Shared infrastructure.
- Release management procedures.
- Cross-project technical coordination.
- Technical documentation.
- Evaluation and benchmarking standards.
- Contributor mentorship.
- Maintainer appointment recommendations.
- Technical continuity and succession.

Core Maintainers shall ensure that computational efficiency, linguistic quality and practical accessibility remain central considerations in technical decision-making.

### 6.3. Appointment

Core Maintainers shall be appointed by the Steering Committee following consultation with relevant technical contributors and working group leads.

Appointments shall identify the scope of responsibility, authority and applicable limitations.

### 6.4. Limitations

Core Maintainers shall not independently:

- Amend the Founding Charter.
- Change project-wide licensing policies.
- Authorize unapproved financial commitments.
- Override applicable data consent requirements.
- Disregard required linguistic validation.
- Establish institutional partnerships requiring contractual obligations.
- Circumvent community governance procedures.

Technical expertise shall not grant unrestricted institutional or financial authority.

## 7. Working groups

### 7.1. Purpose

Working groups shall coordinate defined areas of research, engineering, linguistic development and community operations.

Initial working groups may include:

- AI Research.
- Data and Linguistics.
- Edge AI.
- Community.
- Language-specific working groups.

Additional working groups may be established as the initiative develops.

### 7.2. Establishment

A working group may be proposed through the established public proposal process.

The proposal should identify:

- The purpose and scope of the group.
- Its relationship to the initiative's mission.
- Proposed activities and deliverables.
- Required expertise.
- Proposed leadership.
- Resource requirements.
- Dependencies on other working groups.
- Expected reporting and accountability arrangements.

The Steering Committee shall approve the establishment of official working groups.

### 7.3. Working group leads

Each active working group shall have an identified lead or explicitly documented leadership arrangement.

Working Group Leads shall be responsible for:

- Coordinating activities.
- Maintaining a visible roadmap.
- Supporting contributors.
- Organising reviews and discussions.
- Monitoring deliverables.
- Reporting material risks and dependencies.
- Maintaining relevant documentation.
- Facilitating collaboration with other working groups.
- Recommending additional maintainers where necessary.

Working Group Leads shall be appointed by the Steering Committee following consultation with the relevant contributors and Core Maintainers.

### 7.4. Delegated authority

Working groups may make routine decisions within their approved mandates.

Decisions affecting other working groups, significant resource commitments, project-wide policies or institutional obligations shall be coordinated through the applicable governance process.

### 7.5. Review

Working groups shall periodically review their objectives, progress, leadership and resource requirements.

Inactive or redundant working groups may be restructured, merged or dissolved through a documented Steering Committee decision.

## 8. Language-specific working groups

### 8.1. Purpose

Language-specific working groups shall support the development of technologies and resources for individual languages or related linguistic varieties.

### 8.2. Representation

These groups should include appropriate representation from:

- Native and fluent speakers.
- Linguists and language researchers.
- Data contributors.
- Translators and annotators.
- AI researchers.
- Software and edge-device engineers, where relevant.

The absence of formal academic qualifications shall not invalidate authentic language knowledge or community expertise.

### 8.3. Responsibilities

Language-specific working groups shall contribute to:

- Language representation standards.
- Orthographic and transcription conventions.
- Data collection priorities.
- Annotation guidelines.
- Cultural and contextual validation.
- Dialect and regional coverage.
- Model evaluation.
- Identification of linguistic biases.
- Community engagement.
- Language-specific research priorities.

### 8.4. Decision-making authority

Technical contributors shall not unilaterally determine linguistic or cultural standards without appropriate consultation.

Material decisions concerning language representation shall require participation from the relevant language-specific working group or qualified community representatives.

Where a linguistic or cultural concern raises a credible issue regarding consent, representation or responsible release, publication shall be paused pending documented review.

The Steering Committee may coordinate the resolution of disagreements but shall not override valid legal rights, consent restrictions or applicable data governance obligations.

## 9. Community membership and contributor roles

### 9.1. Community Members

Community Members may:

- Participate in public discussions.
- Provide feedback.
- Submit proposals.
- Request assistance.
- Participate in approved community activities.

Community participation does not automatically confer voting rights, administrative privileges or formal leadership authority.

### 9.2. Contributors

Contributors are individuals or organisations that provide accepted contributions to the initiative.

Contributions may include technical, scientific, linguistic, educational, operational or other mission-aligned activities.

Contributors retain applicable rights to their work under the relevant contribution and licensing policies.

### 9.3. Trusted Contributors

Trusted Contributors are individuals who have demonstrated consistent quality, reliability, responsible participation and adherence to community policies.

Trusted status may support eligibility for delegated operational responsibilities.

It shall not automatically grant unrestricted repository access or institutional authority.

### 9.4. Maintainers

Maintainers hold delegated responsibility for specified repositories, models, datasets, research activities or project components.

Their responsibilities may include:

- Reviewing contributions.
- Managing issues and proposals.
- Maintaining project documentation.
- Coordinating releases.
- Supporting contributors.
- Ensuring compliance with applicable project standards.

The scope of each maintainer's authority shall be documented.

## 10. Contributor progression

Turaco Research shall maintain transparent pathways through which contributors may assume greater responsibility.

Progression shall consider:

- Quality and relevance of contributions.
- Consistency and reliability.
- Demonstrated expertise.
- Responsible communication.
- Collaboration and mentorship.
- Commitment to project policies.
- Ability to fulfil the proposed responsibilities.
- Trust established through documented activities.

Progression shall not be based solely on:

- Number of GitHub commits.
- Public popularity.
- Financial contributions.
- Personal relationships with founders.
- Employment by Clevaway.
- Academic credentials.

Technical and nontechnical contributions shall receive appropriate recognition.

Leadership pathways need not follow a single mandatory sequence.

## 11. Maintainer appointment and review

### 11.1. Nomination

Potential Maintainers may be nominated by existing Maintainers, Working Group Leads, Core Maintainers or themselves.

A nomination shall identify the proposed area of responsibility and supporting evidence of competence and reliability.

### 11.2. Review

The relevant Working Group Lead and a Core Maintainer shall review the nomination.

A standard nomination should allow at least seven calendar days for feedback from relevant contributors.

Any material objections shall be considered before appointment.

### 11.3. Approval

Maintainer appointments shall require approval from the relevant Working Group Lead and an authorized Core Maintainer acting as separate individuals.

Where such roles are not yet independently staffed, the Steering Committee shall exercise the appointment authority.

### 11.4. Initial appointment period

New Maintainers may initially serve a three-month assessment period.

During this period, access and authority should be limited to the responsibilities necessary to evaluate suitability.

Following assessment, the appointment may be confirmed, extended, revised or discontinued through a documented decision.

### 11.5. Maintainer Registry

Turaco Research shall maintain a public Maintainer Registry documenting active leadership appointments.

The registry should include:

- Public contributor identity.
- Assigned role.
- Relevant repository or working group.
- Scope of responsibility.
- Appointment date.
- Appointment status.

Private contact details and sensitive access information shall not be publicly disclosed.

### 11.6. Periodic review

Maintainer appointments shall be reviewed at least every six months.

Reviews shall consider active responsibilities, availability, contribution quality, security requirements and operational needs.

Reviews shall not be used to retaliate against contributors for constructive disagreement or good-faith criticism.

## 12. Decision-making and delegated authority

Decisions shall be made at the lowest appropriate level of authority, provided the responsible individuals possess the necessary expertise and authorization.

Routine decisions within approved working group mandates shall not require committee approval.

Material decisions involving multiple working groups shall require appropriate coordination.

The Steering Committee shall resolve unresolved questions of governance jurisdiction.

Technical decisions shall respect applicable linguistic, data governance, financial, legal and ethical requirements.

### 12.1. Technical decisions

Relevant Maintainers and Working Group Leads may approve routine technical changes within their delegated responsibilities.

Cross-project architectural changes and shared technical standards shall be coordinated by Core Maintainers.

### 12.2. Linguistic decisions

Language representation, transcription conventions, dialect handling and cultural validation shall involve relevant language community representatives.

### 12.3. Official releases

Official model and dataset releases shall follow the applicable release standards.

Required technical, linguistic, licensing, data governance and security approvals shall be obtained before publication.

No individual shall independently bypass a mandatory release requirement.

### 12.4. Institutional and financial decisions

Major partnerships, funding commitments, institutional arrangements and significant expenditures shall require the applicable governance and financial approvals.

Only appropriately authorized persons or legal entities may enter into binding contractual commitments.

Governance approval alone shall not establish legal signing authority.

## 13. Request for Comments process

### 13.1. Purpose

The Request for Comments process, referred to as the RFC process, shall provide a transparent mechanism for proposing and reviewing material changes.

### 13.2. Applicability

An RFC should ordinarily be required for:

- Material governance changes.
- New major research projects.
- Significant architecture changes.
- Project-wide technical standards.
- New language projects requiring substantial resources.
- Material licensing policy changes.
- Significant data governance changes.
- Major institutional restructuring.
- Other decisions identified by the responsible governance authority.

Routine fixes and operational decisions shall not ordinarily require a formal RFC.

### 13.3. Proposal requirements

An RFC should document:

- Title and author.
- Background and motivation.
- Problem being addressed.
- Proposed approach.
- Alternatives considered.
- Expected benefits.
- Risks and limitations.
- Resource implications.
- Impact on existing projects.
- Linguistic, ethical and accessibility considerations, where applicable.
- Proposed implementation and review process.

### 13.4. Public consultation

Standard RFCs shall ordinarily remain open for public feedback for at least fourteen calendar days.

Material changes to the Founding Charter, governance structure or institutional stewardship shall ordinarily receive at least thirty calendar days of public consultation.

The responsible authority may extend consultation where necessary.

Material revisions introduced after consultation shall receive additional review when they substantially change the original proposal.

### 13.5. Resolution

The responsible authority shall consider the proposal, relevant evidence and community feedback.

The final decision shall be recorded as:

- Accepted.
- Rejected.
- Deferred.
- Withdrawn.
- Superseded.

Accepted and rejected proposals shall include an appropriate explanation of the decision.

Public consultation shall inform decision-making without automatically constituting a binding community-wide vote.

## 14. Voting procedures

### 14.1. Consensus

Governance bodies should seek constructive consensus before resorting to formal voting.

Consensus shall not require the indefinite postponement of decisions when reasonable disagreement remains.

### 14.2. Founding-stage decisions

During founding governance, strategic decisions requiring Steering Committee approval shall require the agreement of both founders.

Routine delegated decisions shall remain subject to the agreed founding responsibility portfolios.

Founding-stage deadlocks shall follow the applicable Founders' Agreement.

### 14.3. Quorum

Following expansion of the Steering Committee, a valid decision shall ordinarily require participation by more than half of its active voting membership, with a minimum of two eligible participants.

Members with material conflicts of interest shall not participate in the approval of affected decisions.

If recusals prevent the establishment of a valid quorum, the decision shall be deferred or referred to an independently authorized review mechanism.

### 14.4. Ordinary decisions

Ordinary decisions shall require affirmative votes from more than half of the eligible members participating in a valid vote.

Abstentions shall not count as affirmative votes.

A tied vote shall not be considered approved.

### 14.5. Enhanced approval requirements

The following decisions shall require affirmative votes from at least two-thirds of all eligible Steering Committee members:

- Material amendments to the Governance Framework.
- Material changes to project-wide licensing policies.
- Transitional Steering Committee appointments.
- Removal of a Steering Committee member, subject to independent review.
- Other decisions explicitly designated as requiring enhanced approval.

Amendments to the Founding Charter, dissolution of the initiative and material changes to its institutional stewardship shall require affirmative votes from at least three-quarters of all eligible Steering Committee members.

Any fractional vote requirement shall be rounded upward to the next whole number.

Enhanced approval requirements shall not be reduced through abstention or absence.

Applicable legal, contractual and institutional authorization requirements shall additionally be satisfied.

### 14.6. Voting records

Formal governance decisions shall record:

- The proposal or decision under consideration.
- The applicable decision-making authority.
- Participation and quorum.
- Relevant recusals.
- Voting outcome.
- Final resolution.
- Effective date.
- Responsible implementation parties.

Sensitive information may be withheld where necessary, but the decision and an appropriate explanation shall ordinarily be published.

## 15. Conflicts of interest

Individuals exercising delegated authority shall disclose material actual or potential conflicts of interest.

Conflicts may arise from:

- Employment or commercial relationships.
- Financial interests.
- Sponsorship arrangements.
- Institutional affiliations.
- Personal relationships.
- Intellectual property interests.
- Related-party transactions.

A materially conflicted individual shall not participate in the approval of the affected decision.

They may provide factual information when requested, provided they do not improperly influence the decision.

No individual may independently approve payments, contracts or financial benefits to themselves.

Where all available decision-makers have a material conflict, an appropriately authorized independent review mechanism shall be established before proceeding.

The absence of independent approval shall not justify bypassing this requirement.

## 16. Accountability and reporting

Individuals holding formal responsibilities shall remain accountable for the exercise of their delegated authority.

Turaco Research shall maintain appropriate visibility over:

- Leadership appointments.
- Working group activities.
- Research progress.
- Strategic decisions.
- Policy changes.
- Material risks.
- Resource commitments.
- Financial reports.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Outstanding governance actions.

The Steering Committee should convene at least monthly during active operations.

Working groups should publish periodic progress summaries appropriate to their activities.

Material governance decisions shall be recorded even when discussions occur outside public GitHub platforms.

## 17. Inactivity and resignation

Individuals holding active leadership responsibilities should communicate anticipated periods of unavailability.

A leadership role may be reviewed where an individual has been materially unavailable or unable to fulfil assigned responsibilities for ninety consecutive days without an agreed arrangement.

The review shall consider legitimate personal circumstances, agreed leave and the actual needs of the initiative.

Inactivity shall not automatically constitute misconduct.

Where responsibilities need to be reassigned, the affected individual shall receive appropriate notice and an opportunity to respond.

Individuals may voluntarily resign from active roles through written notice.

Departing leaders shall cooperate in an orderly handover of project responsibilities, documentation and authorized access.

Historical contributions and appropriate recognition shall be preserved.

## 18. Suspension and removal

### 18.1. Grounds for review

Leadership authority may be reviewed where there is credible evidence of:

- Material violation of project policies.
- Abuse of delegated authority.
- Serious security negligence.
- Financial misconduct.
- Undisclosed material conflicts of interest.
- Harassment or discriminatory conduct.
- Persistent failure to fulfil agreed responsibilities.
- Deliberate misrepresentation of research outcomes.
- Unauthorized disclosure or misuse of protected data.
- Other conduct materially harmful to the initiative.

Good-faith disagreement with leadership or criticism of project decisions shall not independently constitute grounds for removal.

### 18.2. Fair procedure

A formal review shall ordinarily include:

- Documentation of the concern.
- Notice to the affected individual.
- An appropriate opportunity to respond.
- Review by individuals without material conflicts of interest.
- Consideration of relevant evidence.
- A documented and proportionate decision.
- Access to the applicable appeal procedure.

### 18.3. Maintainers and Working Group Leads

Maintainer and Working Group Lead appointments may be revised, suspended or revoked by the authority responsible for their appointment, subject to the applicable review procedures.

Appeals may be submitted to the Steering Committee.

### 18.4. Core Maintainers

Removal of a Core Maintainer shall require a documented Steering Committee decision following an appropriate review.

### 18.5. Steering Committee members

Removal of a Steering Committee member shall require independent review and a two-thirds majority of the eligible, non-conflicted committee membership.

The affected member shall not vote on their own removal.

Where sufficient impartial decision-makers are unavailable, the matter shall be referred to an independent review mechanism established under the applicable governance and founding arrangements.

During founding governance, involuntary removal of either founder shall follow the Founders' Agreement and applicable legal obligations.

### 18.6. Access adjustment

Suspension or removal from a role shall result in appropriate adjustment of the access privileges associated with that role.

Such adjustment shall not unnecessarily restrict lawful use of previously released open-source resources or ordinary community participation.

Separate participation restrictions may be imposed where justified under the Code of Conduct or other applicable policies.

## 19. Conflict resolution and appeals

Disagreements shall be addressed constructively and at the appropriate level of responsibility.

The ordinary escalation process shall be:

1. Direct discussion between the relevant parties, where appropriate.
2. Review by the relevant Maintainer or Working Group Lead.
3. Review by Core Maintainers for technical matters.
4. Steering Committee review for unresolved governance matters.
5. Independent mediation or review where internal impartiality cannot be maintained.

Sensitive complaints involving harassment, abuse, security concerns or personal safety may bypass ordinary escalation procedures.

Appeals should ordinarily be submitted within thirty calendar days of notification of a formal decision.

Appeals may identify:

- Material procedural errors.
- Undisclosed conflicts of interest.
- Relevant new evidence.
- Disproportionate sanctions.
- Material inconsistencies with approved policies.

Appeals shall be reviewed by individuals who did not improperly participate in the original decision and who possess no material conflict of interest.

Where an impartial internal appeal body cannot be established, an independently authorized reviewer shall be appointed.

Retaliation against individuals who raise legitimate concerns or participate in good-faith investigations shall not be permitted.

## 20. Emergency authority

Authorized Maintainers, administrators or governance representatives may take temporary protective action where immediate intervention is necessary to address:

- Security vulnerabilities.
- Compromised credentials.
- Unauthorized access.
- Personal data exposure.
- Material licensing or consent concerns.
- Financial misuse.
- Serious operational disruption.
- Other credible threats to project assets or community safety.

Emergency actions shall be limited to what is reasonably necessary to prevent or mitigate harm.

Where practicable, actions shall be documented and communicated to the appropriate governance authority within seventy-two hours.

The responsible authority shall review the action within seven calendar days or document why additional time is necessary.

Emergency authority shall not be used to permanently amend governance policies, remove leadership without due process or circumvent established decision-making requirements.

## 21. Administrative access and operational security

Administrative privileges shall be assigned according to operational requirements and the principle of least privilege.

Governance roles shall remain distinct from technical permissions.

Turaco Research shall maintain appropriate controls over:

- GitHub organisation ownership.
- Repository permissions.
- Model and dataset hosting.
- Computing infrastructure.
- Domain administration.
- Financial administration systems.
- Sensitive research environments.
- Credentials and recovery procedures.

Individuals shall receive only the access necessary for their authorized responsibilities.

Shared personal credentials shall not be used as a substitute for properly delegated access.

Access privileges shall be periodically reviewed and promptly adjusted when responsibilities change.

## 22. Financial governance oversight

The Steering Committee shall provide strategic oversight of the initiative's financial resources.

Detailed procedures for budgeting, expenditure, reimbursement, grants, sponsorship and reporting shall be established through the Financial Governance Policy.

Financial decisions shall respect:

- Approved budgets.
- Applicable donor restrictions.
- Legal and contractual obligations.
- Conflict-of-interest requirements.
- Appropriate approval controls.
- Public accountability.
- Financial separation from unrelated commercial activities.

Until a detailed financial governance policy is adopted, material expenditure commitments and external funding arrangements shall require explicit founding-stage approval and appropriate legal authorization.

Financial support shall not automatically grant governance authority.

## 23. Relationship with Clevaway

Clevaway shall serve as the founding and supporting organisation of Turaco Research in accordance with the approved Clevaway Stewardship Policy.

Clevaway's administrative, financial or technical support shall not independently replace established community governance procedures.

Material disagreements concerning stewardship shall follow the applicable institutional and governance dispute resolution processes.

Community governance authority shall be exercised within applicable legal, contractual and asset ownership arrangements.

## 24. Governance transition and maturity review

Turaco Research shall progressively expand community participation in operational and strategic leadership.

Readiness for governance expansion shall be reviewed at least every six months.

Relevant considerations may include:

- Availability of qualified community representatives.
- Active research and language projects.
- Established working groups.
- Maintainer participation.
- Operational sustainability.
- Governance accountability.
- Financial administration readiness.
- Meaningful representation of language communities.

Within twelve months of adopting this framework, the Steering Committee shall publish a governance maturity assessment.

The assessment shall identify:

- The current governance stage.
- Progress towards broader community leadership.
- Outstanding barriers to expansion.
- Proposed transition arrangements.
- Expected review dates.

Where governance expansion is deferred, the reasons and next review date shall be publicly documented.

The transition to established community governance shall include the adoption of appropriate election, representation and succession procedures.

## 25. Public governance records

Turaco Research shall maintain publicly accessible governance records within its designated community repository.

These records may include:

- The Founding Charter.
- The Governance Framework.
- Approved organisational policies.
- Working Group Mandates.
- Maintainer Registry.
- RFC proposals and decisions.
- Meeting summaries.
- Leadership appointment records.
- Governance maturity assessments.
- Financial reports.
- Policy amendment histories.

Private founding agreements, personal data, security-sensitive information and confidential contractual records shall be maintained through appropriately restricted systems.

Material decisions shall not remain undocumented solely because they were discussed privately or through informal communication channels.

## 26. Policy interpretation

This Governance Framework shall be interpreted consistently with the Founding Charter, applicable stewardship arrangements and approved organisational policies.

Where conflicting policy requirements arise, the Steering Committee shall coordinate their resolution through the established governance process.

No governance provision shall override applicable law, valid third-party rights, existing licenses or binding contractual obligations.

Where a necessary procedure has not yet been adopted, the responsible authority shall establish an appropriate interim procedure consistent with the founding principles before exercising material authority.

## 27. Amendments

Proposed material amendments to this framework shall follow the RFC process.

During founding governance, amendments shall require the approval of both founders.

Following expansion of the Steering Committee, material amendments shall require a two-thirds majority of eligible voting members.

Amendments affecting the Founding Charter or institutional stewardship shall satisfy their additional approval requirements.

Approved amendments shall be version-controlled and shall include an effective date and appropriate explanation.

Amendments shall not be applied retroactively to unfairly disadvantage contributors or invalidate rights previously granted under applicable licenses.

## 28. Adoption

This Governance Framework shall become effective upon approval through the applicable founding and institutional procedures.

By adopting this framework, Turaco Research establishes its commitment to transparent leadership, responsible delegation, meaningful community participation, scientific integrity and sustainable institutional governance.
