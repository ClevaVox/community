# Language-specific Working Group Framework

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Proposed

## 1. Purpose

Language-specific working groups shall coordinate research, linguistic resources and technology development for individual languages or related language varieties.

They provide a mechanism through which native speakers, linguists, researchers and engineers can collaboratively develop technologies addressing the needs of specific linguistic communities.

## 2. Establishment criteria

A language-specific working group may be proposed when:

- A language or linguistic variety is inadequately represented in existing technologies.
- There is an identifiable research or accessibility need.
- Relevant linguistic expertise or community participation can be established.
- Potential data sources or collection opportunities exist.
- Proposed activities are consistent with the Founding Charter.
- Appropriate leadership and coordination arrangements can be identified.

The absence of existing datasets shall not independently disqualify a language from consideration.

## 3. Language project proposal

A proposal should identify:

- Language name and alternative names.
- Language identifier, where available.
- Geographical and linguistic context.
- Relevant dialects and language varieties.
- Existing research and technologies.
- Community needs and intended use cases.
- Available or potential datasets.
- Proposed research priorities.
- Community representation arrangements.
- Expected computational and deployment requirements.
- Proposed leadership.
- Resource requirements.
- Known limitations and risks.

Proposals shall follow the applicable governance and RFC procedures.

## 4. Composition

A language-specific working group may include:

- Native and fluent speakers.
- Linguists.
- Translators.
- Data contributors and annotators.
- AI researchers.
- Software engineers.
- Edge AI engineers.
- Community contributors.
- Academic or institutional collaborators.

Membership shall be based on relevant expertise, participation and commitment rather than exclusively on technical qualifications.

## 5. Responsibilities

Language-specific working groups shall coordinate:

- Language representation requirements.
- Language-specific research priorities.
- Data collection needs.
- Transcription and annotation conventions.
- Orthographic and pronunciation documentation.
- Cultural and contextual validation.
- Dialect and regional coverage.
- Linguistic evaluation.
- Community engagement.
- Practical model use cases.
- Language-specific feedback and improvement proposals.

## 6. Cross-group collaboration

Language-specific working groups shall collaborate with the general working groups.

AI Research shall support model development and scientific evaluation.

Data and Linguistics shall support dataset engineering, consent, linguistic validation and data quality.

Edge AI shall support computational efficiency, optimization and device deployment.

Community shall support contributor participation, outreach and educational activities.

## 7. Linguistic representation

Language-specific working groups shall respect authentic linguistic variation and cultural context.

Material decisions concerning language representation shall involve appropriate community participation.

Where multiple linguistic conventions exist, the working group should document the alternatives and adopt approaches that preserve relevant diversity.

A working group shall not claim to represent every speaker or possess exclusive authority over a language.

## 8. Expected deliverables

Deliverables may include:

- Language research profiles.
- Linguistic reference documentation.
- Data collection guidelines.
- Annotation standards.
- Validated linguistic resources.
- Language-specific evaluation datasets.
- Research proposals.
- Model evaluation reports.
- Community feedback summaries.
- Language development roadmaps.

## 9. Leadership and authority

Language-specific working groups shall have an approved leadership arrangement documented in the Maintainer Registry.

Delegated authority shall follow the Governance Framework and approved working group mandate.

Credible concerns relating to consent, cultural sensitivity, linguistic representation or responsible release shall be documented and reviewed through the applicable procedures.

## 10. Reporting and review

Active language-specific working groups shall maintain a visible roadmap and periodic progress summaries.

Working groups should review their language coverage, community participation, research priorities and outstanding data requirements regularly.
